# Cyvexly Studio — Website Vision Plan

**Status:** Creative and business vision for the first public website  
**Prepared:** August 30, 2026  
**Owner launch direction updated:** September 4, 2026
**Scope of this document:** What the website must contain, communicate, and enable. This is intentionally not a technical build guide.

---

## 1. The vision in one page

Cyvexly Studio will be an independent, remote web design and development studio serving businesses across the United States at launch. It will not be a self-service website workshop, template marketplace, or drag-and-drop builder. Prospective clients will describe their business, goals, preferred style, pages, features, content readiness, budget, and timing. Cyvexly will review that brief, recommend the right scope, and send a clear proposal.

The site must make five things obvious within the first minute:

1. **What Cyvexly does:** strategy, design, development, launch, and ongoing website care.
2. **Who it is for:** service businesses, professionals, startups, creators, and online stores that want a serious business website.
3. **Why it is credible:** thoughtful process, clearly defined deliverables, visible work, transparent starting prices, and no false claims.
4. **What it may cost:** clear starting packages, useful add-ons, and custom quoting where complexity demands it.
5. **What to do next:** complete the Project Planner and receive a response within two business days.

### Recommended launch message

**Eyebrow:** Independent web studio · Serving businesses across the United States
**Headline:** **Websites built to make your business unmistakable.**  
**Supporting copy:** Cyvexly Studio plans, designs, builds, and supports custom websites for businesses ready to look credible, work smarter, and turn more visits into action.  
**Primary action:** Describe your project  
**Secondary action:** Explore services

### Brand promise

> A clear process, a distinctive website, and no mystery about what happens next.

### Recommended market position

Cyvexly should sit between low-cost template freelancers and large agencies:

- more custom, accountable, and strategically guided than a bargain template offer;
- more accessible and personal than a large agency engagement;
- visually experimental on its own site, but commercially practical in how it describes client work;
- confident without claiming awards, clients, results, or years of experience that do not yet exist.

## 2. What comparable studios reveal

The strongest studio websites repeatedly use the same trust structure, even when their visual styles differ.

| Pattern seen in the market | What Cyvexly should adopt | Cyvexly expression |
|---|---|---|
| A short, outcome-led hero | Say what changes for the client, not just “we make websites” | “Websites built to make your business unmistakable.” |
| Work appears early | Let visitors judge taste and relevance quickly | Featured work immediately after a short credibility strip |
| Services are grouped by client need | Keep the offer understandable | Launch, Business, Growth, Commerce, plus focused add-ons |
| Process is visible | Reduce fear about cost, delays, and revisions | A five-stage “from signal to launch” timeline |
| Pricing is either transparent or clearly scoped after discovery | Give an honest budget frame without turning the service into a commodity | Published starting packages plus a custom Project Planner |
| Case studies tell a problem-to-outcome story | Do more than show attractive screenshots | Challenge, goals, decisions, deliverables, final experience, and real results when available |
| Contact forms qualify the opportunity | Ask about goals, scope, budget, timeline, assets, and decision-makers | A conversational multi-step Project Planner |
| FAQ removes buying objections | Answer timing, ownership, revisions, content, hosting, and payment questions | FAQ repeated where it helps: Pricing, Process, and Planner |
| Ongoing support is visible | Make launch the start of a relationship | Optional care plans with defined response times and monthly capacity |

Reference observations:

- [Ramotion's service page](https://www.ramotion.com/web-design/) exposes services, process, industries, benefits, FAQ, insights, proof, and a strong contact path on one long-form page. It also frames scope around page count, UX complexity, CMS, content, integrations, motion, and development.
- [Designjoy](https://www.designjoy.co/) makes its model, request flow, turnaround, work, price, and FAQ immediately understandable. Cyvexly should borrow the clarity, not the subscription-only offer.
- [COLLINS](https://wearecollins.com/) leads with a strong point of view and highly visual work. Cyvexly should use the same confidence while keeping its services and buying path more explicit.
- [Clay's 2026 agency guidance](https://clay.global/blog/best-web-design-agencies/professional-agency) emphasizes fast, responsive, intuitive, secure, maintainable websites rather than aesthetics alone.
- Project inquiry patterns consistently request the business goal, audience, page count, functionality, content/brand readiness, inspiration, budget, and timeline. [Paperform's web-design intake summary](https://paperform.co/templates/web-design-project-inquiry-form/) and this [detailed project questionnaire](https://mybrandingagency.com/website-questionnaire/) both reflect that pattern.

### Strategic conclusion

Cyvexly should not imitate one agency. It should combine:

- the confidence and visual restraint of a premium studio;
- the plain-language offer clarity of a productized service;
- the scope discipline of a professional agency;
- the personal access and flexibility of an independent designer.

## 3. Audience and desired actions

### Primary clients

1. **New businesses needing a credible first site**  
   They need structure, brand direction, copy guidance, lead capture, and a professional launch.

2. **Established small businesses with an outdated site**  
   They need better presentation, mobile usability, clearer services, stronger calls to action, and easier updates.

3. **Professional and creative service providers**  
   Consultants, agencies, contractors, wellness providers, legal/accounting practices, photographers, and similar businesses need authority, portfolios, booking, and inquiry flows.

4. **Startups and digital products**  
   They need a launch or marketing site, product explanation, waitlist/demo conversion, CMS content, and integrations.

5. **Small online stores**  
   They need product presentation, commerce setup, payment flows, shipping/tax planning, policies, and training.

### Not the initial focus

- complex custom software platforms;
- regulated enterprise systems requiring a large multidisciplinary team;
- high-volume marketplaces;
- emergency “tomorrow” launches;
- speculative work or payment based only on future revenue;
- a customer-controlled page builder inside Cyvexly's own site.

### Primary conversion

**Submit a qualified project brief.**

### Secondary conversions

- view relevant work;
- understand package starting points;
- review the process;
- ask a short pre-sales question;
- return later through a saved Project Planner link.

## 4. Brand identity and creative direction

### Personality

Cyvexly should feel:

- futuristic, not gimmicky;
- sharp, not cold;
- inventive, not chaotic;
- premium, not corporate;
- clear, not jargon-heavy;
- personal, not like an anonymous production line.

### Voice

Use short, active, confident sentences. Explain outcomes in ordinary business language. Technical ability should be visible through the quality of the experience, not through walls of tool names.

**Say:** “Tell us what the site needs to accomplish.”  
**Avoid:** “Leverage a synergistic digital transformation ecosystem.”

**Say:** “You will know the scope, price, and milestones before work begins.”  
**Avoid:** “We make all your dreams come true.”

### Visual theme: cyber new-age tech

The visual system should suggest a living signal network inside a bright arctic atmosphere: luminous cyber-blue energy, precision grids, fine orbital lines, data traces, cool smoke, and translucent glass surfaces. The site should feel like the control surface of a forward-looking studio, not a gaming clan, crypto exchange, or “Matrix” imitation.

#### Color system

| Role | Direction | Suggested value |
|---|---|---|
| Arctic mist | Main background | `#EEF4FA` |
| Ice field | Alternating background | `#E3EBF3` |
| Frosted glass | Cards and elevated sections | translucent `#F8FBFF` |
| Smoke glass | Stronger glass surface | translucent `#D8E1EA` |
| Midnight slate | Primary text | `#10213D` |
| Cool graphite | Secondary text | `#526176` |
| Cyber blue | Main brand/action accent | `#1478FF` |
| Ion cyan | Light and interactive highlight | `#36C7FF` |
| Signal emerald | Small success or proof accent only | `#16B777` |
| Warning coral | Errors and urgent status only | `#D9435F` |

Bright colors should be used as signals, edges, glows, and short highlights—not as long paragraphs. White space should feel like cool atmospheric space, with enough smoky gray to keep the interface dimensional rather than sterile.

#### Typography direction

- **Display:** a geometric, modern grotesk with unusual but readable forms; Space Grotesk or Sora are good references.
- **Body/UI:** a highly readable sans serif such as Inter or Manrope.
- **Utility labels:** a restrained monospace such as JetBrains Mono for step numbers, metadata, filters, and system labels.
- Headlines should be large and tightly composed; body copy should remain generous and readable.

#### Logo direction

- Primary: a clean **Cyvexly Studio** wordmark.
- Secondary: an abstract **C/Y signal mark** built from two connected paths or orbit lines.
- The logo must work as one color before any glow is added.
- Avoid shields, generic code brackets, globes, mouse cursors, and circuit-brain clichés.

#### Image and art direction

- finished client websites shown in clean device-free crops or restrained browser frames;
- abstract dimensional forms used only to support the content hierarchy;
- macro textures resembling smoked glass, brushed metal, spectral light, and atmospheric particles;
- custom diagrams for process and service relationships;
- no random stock photos of people pointing at laptops;
- no fake dashboards or unreadable pseudo-code as decoration.

#### Motion direction

- slow ambient blue-gray gradient drift in the hero;
- fine line traces that respond gently to pointer movement;
- card edges that activate on hover;
- staggered reveal of portfolio details;
- step transitions in the Project Planner;
- a visible reduced-motion version with no required information hidden behind animation.

### 6.7 Process

**Purpose:** Make the engagement feel organized, collaborative, and low-risk.

**Five client-facing stages:**

1. **Brief & fit**  
   The client completes the Planner. Cyvexly reviews the goals, scope, content, timing, and budget, then confirms whether the project is a fit.

2. **Direction & scope**  
   The client receives a recommended sitemap, deliverables, timeline, price, payment milestones, responsibilities, and agreement.

3. **Design & approval**  
   The project establishes a visual direction, then expands into agreed pages and states. Feedback is gathered at defined checkpoints.

4. **Build & quality review**  
   The working experience is completed, content is placed, and the client reviews the responsive site. Required functionality, accessibility, forms, and content are checked.

5. **Launch & care**  
   The site is approved, launched, handed over, and supported through the included launch window. Ongoing care is optional.

**For each stage show:** client inputs, Cyvexly deliverables, the approval point, and the expected range of time.

**Trust panel:** “You will always know what is waiting on Cyvexly, what is waiting on you, and what happens next.”

## 7. Recommended public pricing

### Market grounding

Current published pricing is extremely broad. [Clutch's August 2026 guide](https://clutch.co/web-designers/pricing) reports that most reviewed projects are under $10,000, while the typical listed agency rate is $100–$149 per hour and total projects can range from $2,000 to $100,000 depending on scope. [WebFX's 2026 data](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/pricing/) places average web-design work around $1,000–$30,000+, with basic small-business style work around $2,000–$5,000. A [2026 freelancer pricing analysis](https://agentsite.ai/blog/how-much-to-charge-for-a-website) recommends roughly $2,500–$4,000 for a five-to-seven-page first-stage small-business offer.

Cyvexly's launch pricing should therefore be accessible but not “cheap website” pricing. The recommendations below position it as a serious independent studio and leave room for proper discovery, revisions, quality review, and support.

### Website packages

| Package | Recommended starting price | Best for | Included scope | Typical window |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Signal** | **$1,800** | A focused first presence, campaign, event, or single service | 1–3 core pages, light visual direction, responsive design, one primary form, essential search setup, analytics connection, two review rounds, launch support | 2–3 weeks |
| **Orbit** | **$3,500** | A complete small-business or professional-services website | Up to 7 core pages, custom visual system, conversion-focused page structure, two forms, basic CMS or blog, essential search setup, analytics, two review rounds, owner handoff, launch support | 4–6 weeks |
| **Nexus** | **$5,800** | A growing business, content-led site, or strategic redesign | Up to 12 core pages, deeper strategy, custom components, CMS collections, up to two standard integrations, content migration allowance, enhanced motion direction, essential search setup, analytics, three review rounds, training, launch support | 6–9 weeks |
| **Commerce** | **From $8,500** | A small-to-medium online store or product business | Store structure, product and collection templates, initial catalog allowance, checkout/payment configuration, shipping/tax requirement review, policy templates/placement, transactional experience review, analytics, training, launch support | 8–14+ weeks |
| **Custom system** | **Quoted after discovery** | Memberships, complex booking, multilingual, large migrations, advanced integrations, or unusual workflows | A scoped proposal with phases, acceptance points, timeline, dependencies, and milestone billing | Scope dependent |

**Core pages** means distinct content templates or page layouts, not every automatically generated CMS item or product record. Final proposals must define the page/template count in plain language.

### What every website project includes

- a defined goal and primary action;
- sitemap/page plan;
- responsive desktop, tablet, and mobile behavior;
- custom styling aligned to the client's brand;
- accessible interaction and content standards target;
- functional forms and clear confirmation/error states;
- baseline page titles and descriptions for agreed pages;
- favicon/social-sharing image direction;
- analytics connection when the client supplies or authorizes the account;
- pre-launch review and client approval;
- handoff/ownership terms;
- 14 days of post-launch defect support.

### Founding-client offer

For the first three to five qualified projects, use a private founding-client adjustment of up to 15% in exchange for permission to publish a truthful case study and request an honest review. Do not cover a weak offer with a permanent fake sale or countdown timer.

### Costs that must be distinguished from Cyvexly's fee

- domain registration and renewal;
- hosting/platform subscription;
- premium fonts, stock assets, licensed themes, apps, or plugins;
- payment-processing fees;
- third-party email, booking, CRM, commerce, or analytics subscriptions;
- taxes where applicable;
- translation, photography, video, and specialized legal/compliance work unless specifically included.

## 8. Payments, proposals, and commercial expectations

**Owner decision, September 4, 2026:** payment-provider selection and payment
integration are tabled for separate research. The options below remain research
and planning guidance, not an authorization to activate Stripe, PayPal, Venmo,
ACH, installments, or any other provider. Until a provider and accounts are
approved, the public site must not state or imply that a specific payment method
is presently operational. The Project Planner remains inquiry-only and requests
no payment. Professional proposals, agreements, invoices, and milestone
payments remain the intended commercial model after the provider decision.

### Recommended payment experience

Use professional invoices after scope approval. The website may explain payment methods, but it should not ask a new prospect to buy a full custom website before Cyvexly has reviewed the project.

**Research candidate:** Stripe invoices or hosted payment pages.
**Research candidate:** PayPal invoicing for clients who prefer PayPal or Venmo.
**Research candidate:** bank transfer for approved invoices.

Stripe currently supports cards, bank debits/transfers, wallets, local methods, and eligible buy-now-pay-later options through its payment system. Its U.S. standard pricing page lists 2.9% + $0.30 for domestic cards and 0.8% capped at $5 for ACH Direct Debit; fees can change, so the live provider page remains authoritative. See [Stripe payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/overview), [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing), and [ACH invoicing guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/ach-direct-debit). [PayPal Invoicing](https://www.paypal.com/us/business/accept-payments/invoice) supports PayPal, Venmo in the U.S., cards, Apple Pay, Pay Later, and bank payment options, including for customers without PayPal accounts.

### Methods to evaluate after the Owner resumes payment research

- Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, and other processor-supported cards;
- Apple Pay and Google Pay where available;
- ACH bank payment for U.S. clients;
- PayPal and U.S. Venmo through PayPal invoicing;
- bank transfer by arrangement;

## 13. Quality, accessibility, and experience requirements

The cyber aesthetic must not reduce usability.

### Accessibility target

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** across the public experience. The [W3C WCAG 2.2 standard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) requires keyboard operability and includes contrast and navigation expectations; the design must preserve visible focus, readable type, sufficient targets, labels, instructions, and meaningful error messages.

Required experience qualities:

- all functions available by keyboard;
- visible focus that is not hidden by sticky interface elements;
- sufficient contrast without relying on glow;
- text remains readable when enlarged;
- meaningful link/button language;
- labels persist for form fields;
- errors identify what happened and how to correct it;
- animation does not block content and respects reduced motion;
- images have meaningful text alternatives when they convey information;
- decorative visuals stay ignored by assistive technology;
- video has captions/transcripts if used;
- mobile layouts preserve the same information and actions as desktop.

### Performance expectation

The site should feel immediate despite its visual personality. Set a quality goal around the current stable Core Web Vitals—LCP, INP, and CLS—as described by [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals). Large effects, media, and motion must earn their place.

### Form and payment safety

- collect only information needed for the project inquiry;
- never ask prospects to enter passwords or raw payment credentials into Cyvexly's own forms;
- use secure provider-hosted payment pages or components;
- separate marketing consent from the permission needed to answer an inquiry;
- make spam protection accessible;
- provide retention/contact language in the Privacy Policy.
